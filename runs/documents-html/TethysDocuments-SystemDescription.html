Tethys Retrieval — System Description

Tethys Retrieval is a fictional asteroid-retrieval mission: the solar-electric tug Argent flies to the small near-Earth asteroid 2031 QX Amaranth, characterises it with the free-flying inspector Wren, captures and despins it, and returns it to a lunar distant retrograde orbit for the visiting vehicle Lantern. Every name and number is invented; the physics is not.

Mission requirements

TR-001
The mission shall deliver the target body 2031 QX, intact and with a mass of at least 100 t, into the designated lunar distant retrograde orbit.
TR-002
The mission shall complete the insertion of the target into the distant retrograde orbit within 2 200 days of launch (TethysMission::tethys.nominalDuration).
TR-003
Before committing to capture, the mission shall determine the target's shape, mass to within 10 % and spin state, and shall confirm that each lies within the capture system's design envelope.
TR-004
The flight system shall be compatible with the Arcturus-5H launch vehicle and its direct injection to an Earth-escape trajectory.
TR-005
The flight system shall communicate through the Meridian deep-space network at every Earth range of the mission.
TR-006
The target body shall be placed in an orbit that remains bound to the Moon for at least 100 years without station-keeping, and no credible failure during the return shall place it on an Earth-impact trajectory.
TR-007
The flight system shall carry no single-point failure that ends the mission.
TR-008
While attached to the target, the mission shall demonstrate a slow-push deflection and shall measure the resulting change in the target's heliocentric velocity to an accuracy of 1 mm/s.
TR-009
After delivery, the tug shall hold the target in an attitude and with a docking interface that lets the visiting vehicle Lantern dock and reach the target's surface.
